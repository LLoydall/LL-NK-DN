* **Called capital:** The funds requested from an investor, against which management fees can be counted depending on specific agreements[cite: 1].
* **CRS (Common Reporting Standard):** A global standard developed by the OECD for the automatic exchange of financial account information between countries to combat tax evasion. In the context of fund administration, CRS data extraction represents one of the smaller administrative workflows handled for clients[cite: 2].
* **Foot (footing):** A quality control check to ensure numbers reconcile, such as verifying that a balance sheet relates correctly to an equity balance[cite: 1]. Ensuring numbers foot is a crucial step to catch errors in financial reporting[cite: 5].
* **Fund administrator:** An outsourced provider responsible for preparing financial reports, such as half-year valuations[cite: 1]. These providers typically bundle their services into a framework agreement[cite: 2].
* **General partner:** The entity managing a fund, operating alongside the investors[cite: 1]. US general partners frequently explore replacing their existing outsourced providers[cite: 2].
* **Management fee:** An ongoing charge that can be structured to count against an investor's called capital[cite: 1].
* **NAV (Net Asset Value):** A financial workflow prepared by a fund administrator that represents the valuation of a fund[cite: 1]. According to the transcript in "call-1-nav-workflow-review.pdf", finalizing a NAV often takes six or seven iteration loops to correct errors[cite: 1, 5].
* **Portfolio company:** A business invested in by a fund[cite: 1]. Information and activities regarding portfolio companies must be accurately reflected in financial statements[cite: 1].
* **Side letter:** A specialized investor agreement that outlines custom terms, such as specific management fee calculations[cite: 1].
* **Statement of operations:** A financial reporting document that can require significant time and iteration to finalize accurately[cite: 1].
* **Subsequent event:** A deal or transaction that takes place between the fiscal close period and the issuance of the financial statements[cite: 1]. Proper accounting requires moving these events into the portfolio section for the following year[cite: 1].
